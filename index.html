01 · Problem identification

The product isn't just a cart.
It's coordination.

Picking a restaurant, collecting everyone's items, waiting for replies and chasing small UPI payments create friction before and after checkout.

Anchor persona

Hostel / PG flatmates

“Everyone's hungry. Nobody wants to coordinate.”

3–8 students, late-night cravings, shared rooms and a dense social graph. The host often becomes both coordinator and payer.

Settlement

02 · Market research

Everyone shipped a shared link.
The gap is what happens next.

Competitor analysis points to an opportunity beyond basic shared-cart mechanics: real-time coordination and fairer payment handling.

Swiggy

Launch-weekend reporting indicated group ordering reached 6% of orders and group AOV was 3.7× the usual average. These are launch-window figures, not steady-state benchmarks.

Zomato

The existing flow is framed as host-led and single-payer, without a confirmed real-time presence layer. Competitor specifics may vary by source and market.

Uber Eats / DoorDash

Deadlines, spend limits and host controls offer useful patterns, while fee fairness may remain incomplete depending on market and version.

Grab / Meituan

Split mechanics and group-buying offer adjacent patterns for settlement fairness and future price-sensitive use cases.

Gaming lobbies

03 · Root-cause diagnosis

Is it demand, discovery
or the product?

The diagnosis treats coordination as the primary product problem, with discovery as a secondary friction point.

Product thesis

Move the entry point into the channel where group intent already lives, then turn the messy conversation into a live, structured cart.

04 · Solution design

One link. One live cart.
Less chasing.

Stage 1 focuses on collaborative coordination and host-pay checkout. Split-pay and fulfilment enhancements follow after the core flow is validated.

Today · fragmented flow

  1. Debate restaurant in a group chat.
  2. Pass one phone around to collect items.
  3. Lose track of who added what.
  4. Host pays and shares screenshots.
  5. Chase repayments after delivery.

Proposed · collaborative flow

  1. Share a WhatsApp link, QR or room code.
  2. Everyone joins from their own device.
  3. Each person adds to a colour-coded sub-cart.
  4. Presence and ready-check show group progress.
  5. Timer fires confirmed items; host pays in Stage 1.

Host view · orchestration

Conceptual UI based on the PRD requirements, not a production Zomato screen.

  • Live member presence and item ownership
  • Per-person subtotals and group total
  • Ready tally and countdown timer
  • Nudge, lock cart, remove member and transfer host
  • Place confirmed items when timer expires

05 · Prioritization

Ship the differentiator first.

The collaborative cart is the highest-scoring feature in the RICE assessment, despite higher implementation effort.

In scope · MVP

Single restaurant and address, host-pay, real-time presence, sub-carts, ready-check, timer, nudges, QR/link/room code and no-install invitee web cart.

Out of scope · deliberate trade-offs

Multi-restaurant, multi-address, split-pay, labeled sub-bags and Gold-gating. These add complexity or weaken the acquisition loop before the core mechanic is proven.

08 · Rollout roadmap

Prove coordination.
Then add fairness and delight.

Each stage has an explicit gate so the team can learn before expanding scope.

STAGE 1 · 0–3 MONTHS

Coordination-first MVP

Presence avatars, per-person sub-carts, live totals, activity feed, ready-check, timer, nudges and no-install invitee cart. Advance when adoption reaches the 3–5% target and at least 60% of initiated carts fire.

STAGE 2 · 3–6 MONTHS

Settlement & consensus

UPI-collect split-pay, itemized invoices and pre-cart restaurant voting. Validate split-pay attach and reduce nudges per order.

STAGE 3 · 6–12 MONTHS

Fulfilment delight

Pilot labeled sub-bags with high-volume restaurant partners and large-order vehicle logic. Track on-time delivery and cohort satisfaction.

09 · The prize

Turn a one-off group order
into a repeatable habit.

The opportunity is not only a larger basket. It is reducing coordination friction enough that groups choose to order together again.

Desirability

Reduce phone-passing, uncertainty and repayment chasing.

Feasibility

Ship the real-time cart first and measure sync reliability.

Viability

Use a controlled MVP experiment to test incremental penetration before committing to the full roadmap.
